## Beginner

Prompt: I am new to probability. A bag has three blue counters and one gold. Each counter is equally likely to be chosen. Help me find the probability of gold, one small step at a time. Do not give me the answer yet.

Actual response:

**Step 1 – Count the counters**
The bag contains three blue counters and one gold counter. To find the total number of possible outcomes, simply add them up.

**Your turn:** How many counters are there in total?

## Misconception

Prompt: I think P(gold) is 1/3 because there are three blue counters and one gold. Each counter is equally likely. Is my reasoning correct? Explain simply and ask one question to help me fix it.

Actual response:

**Verdict:** Partly correct.  
**What you got:** You recognized that each counter is equally likely.  
**Missing point:** You need to count *all* counters, not just the blue ones. There are 3 blue + 1 gold = 4 counters total.  
**Better answer:** The probability of drawing the gold counter is \(\frac{1}{4}\).  
**Quick check:** How many counters are there in total?

## Advanced

Prompt: I understand basic probability. Explain why favourable outcomes divided by total outcomes fails when outcomes are not equally likely. Give one short numerical counterexample and a question to check my understanding.

Actual response:

**Why the simple ratio can fail**
When outcomes have different probabilities, counting them equally misrepresents the true chance. The correct probability is the sum of the individual probabilities of the favourable outcomes, not merely their count.

**Counterexample**
Consider a biased coin where \(P(\text{Heads}) = 0.8\) and \(P(\text{Tails}) = 0.2\). Counting outcomes gives \(\frac{1}{2}\), but the real probability of heads is \(0.8\).

**Check your understanding**
A six‑sided die is weighted so that face \(6\) occurs with probability \(0.5\), and the other five faces share the remaining \(0.5\) equally. What is the probability of rolling an even number? *Write the expression you would use before calculating the numeric value.*
